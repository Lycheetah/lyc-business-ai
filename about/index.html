Why this exists

Useful business AI should fit the business, not ask the business to become a lab.

I’m Mackenzie Clark, building LYC from New Zealand. I started it because many businesses have the same practical problem: valuable time is lost copying information, checking details, chasing answers and preparing work that still needs a person to approve.

A generic chatbot can produce fluent text, but it does not automatically know a company’s rules, exceptions, sources or authority boundaries. LYC is my attempt to make the useful part more grounded: understand one real workflow, preserve the business truth, build a focused specialist, and test it before asking anyone to rely on it.

That means starting narrow. No forced platform migration, no model-vendor lock-in as the product, and no promise that an agent should act alone. The business decides what is true, what is allowed and whether the evidence justifies continuing.

The work behind the product

A repeatable build loop, not a one-shot prompt.

LYC combines workflow discovery, business knowledge, deterministic rules, model assistance, evaluation and human review into one inspectable path.

How I work

Four principles keep the work useful.

Start with reality

The workflow comes before the model. If the actual process is unclear, the right next step is discovery—not a larger promise.

Keep authority visible

The system can structure, retrieve, calculate and draft. The business owner keeps approval over rules, commitments and consequential actions.

Make uncertainty useful

Missing information, conflicting sources and unusual cases become questions or safe stops instead of confident guesses.

Earn the next step

A pilot should produce evidence about time, corrections, failures and usefulness before anyone widens the scope.

Where LYC is now

Early, deliberate and ready for the next real conversation.

The platform and three fictional business witnesses are being used to pressure-test the build process with synthetic data. The public site is a commercial prototype, not proof of customer results.

The first real step is a permission-first conversation with a business owner about one painful workflow. There are no customer case studies or savings claims yet. If a workflow is a fit, the first experiment stays draft-first, measurable and human reviewed.
