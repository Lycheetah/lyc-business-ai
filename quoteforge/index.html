QuoteForge

Spend less time rebuilding the same quote from scratch.

QuoteForge is a trade-specific quoting agent being developed to turn customer enquiries into structured, evidence-backed preliminary estimates and drafts using the business's approved pricing and rules.

Pilot Preparation

QuoteForge is currently in active development and internal testing. We are preparing it for controlled founding pilots rather than presenting it as a finished autonomous quoting platform.

How pricing works

Deterministic code owns the arithmetic

When a price can be calculated from approved rules, code does the calculation — not the language model. The model may structure an inquiry, identify missing information, or ask clarification questions, but it does not invent the pricebook, approve the quote, or send it.

The model does

  • Structure customer inquiries
  • Identify missing information
  • Ask clarification questions
  • Retrieve approved business rules
  • Prepare structured drafts

The model does not do

  • Calculate prices from memory
  • Invent pricing rules
  • Approve quotes
  • Send commitments to customers
  • Modify the master pricebook

Designed around real quoting friction

Common problems QuoteForge is being designed around include:

Incomplete customer messages
Repeated follow-up to clarify basics
Quoting from memory or stale spreadsheets

Stale pricing across different jobs

How QuoteForge processes an enquiry

When a customer sends an incomplete message, the system identifies what is missing, asks relevant questions, structures the scope and retrieves approved business rules.

Customer

“Need a fence replaced, probably around 15 metres and maybe a gate.”

The system then:

  1. Identifies missing information
  2. Asks relevant clarification questions
  3. Structures the job scope
  4. Retrieves approved business rules
  5. Sends deterministic inputs to quote engine
  6. Displays unknowns clearly
  7. Creates preliminary output
  8. Requests owner review

What we will measure in the pilot

  • Inquiry completeness — how much of the required information was captured
  • Time from inquiry to review-ready draft
  • Number of clarification turns needed
  • Owner correction rate — how often the draft needed changes
  • Unsupported price or promise count — times the system attempted something outside its rules
  • Escalation and safe-stop rate — times the system correctly identified uncertainty
  • Staff time spent per inquiry
  • Approval or rejection reasons
  • Incidents, reversals, and lessons learned
