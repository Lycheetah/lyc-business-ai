Approved information

Business facts come from approved sources — not from the model's training data or general internet knowledge. When an agent references a price, a policy or a service rule, it retrieves that information from the business's approved knowledge base.

Human authority

Important commitments stay with the right person. A draft quote is not a binding commitment. An AI-prepared document is not an approved document. Consequential decisions remain under human control by default.

Deterministic rules

Exact calculations and hard rules stay in software where possible. If code can calculate a price accurately, we do not ask the language model to guess. This keeps arithmetic reliable and audit trails clear.

Explicit permissions

Each workflow connection receives only the capabilities it needs. An agent that drafts quotes does not also have access to accounting systems. Permissions are explicit, scoped and revocable.

Visible uncertainty

Unknowns stay unknown. When information is missing, ambiguous or outside the approved knowledge base, the system says so. It does not guess, fill gaps with plausible text or present uncertainty as fact.

Reviewable activity

The system records requests, tools used and observed outcomes where appropriate. This creates an audit trail that the business can review, not a black box that produces results without explanation.

Revocation

Agents and connections can be disabled without asking the model. The business retains direct control over what the system can access and do, regardless of what the AI suggests.

Data handling

Data routes, providers, retention and permissions are reviewed for the specific workflow before customer information is admitted. Customer-specific deployments may require privacy-impact, contractual and security review appropriate to the information involved.

What we do when the answer is uncertain

The customer did not provide enough detail

The agent asks specific clarification questions before proceeding. It does not assume, fill gaps with plausible text or present an incomplete scope as complete.

The approved knowledge base does not cover the situation

The agent says so explicitly and escalates to the business owner. It does not improvise a rule or retrieve unapproved information.

The calculation depends on unknown variables

The agent flags the unknowns, shows what is verified and what is not, and presents the draft as preliminary — not final.

The request falls outside the configured workflow

The agent stops and says it cannot help with that request within its current permissions. It does not attempt to operate outside its boundaries.

Principles we hold

The model does not decide its own authority.

Missing information stays missing until verified.

A draft is not silently turned into a commitment.

Every workflow gets explicit limits.

The business decides what becomes canonical.

Human review is used where consequence justifies it.
