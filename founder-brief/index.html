A founder's brief

Useful AI should not cost an enterprise ransom.

I spent two years learning, experimenting, and building before asking anyone to buy. LYC Business AI is the commercial expression of that work: controlled AI specialists for real business workflows, priced so a normal New Zealand business can test one without betting the farm.

The simple version

This is a business system, not a clever chat window.

The model is only one part. The valuable work is turning a company's rules, documents, exceptions, approvals, and limits into a workflow that can help without quietly taking over.

A real commercial product surface

A working Next.js site with a clear offer, pricing, trust, privacy, terms, industries, and contact journey — not just a chatbot demo.

A reusable agent foundation

Provider-neutral controls for approved knowledge, workflow boundaries, permissions, safe stops, evaluation, and a record of what happened.

A first specialist: QuoteForge

A quoting workflow that structures incomplete enquiries and prepares review-ready drafts while the business owner keeps pricing and approval authority.

Commercial discipline

Fixed starting prices, visible usage limits, no surprise overages, human approval, and a day-30 evidence review before expansion.

Why this matters to me

This is not a sympathy story. It is the result of finding a path.

I did not start with a formal coding, technology, or AI background. I spent two years learning in public and experimenting in private, without making money the centre of the work.

That time built more than tool knowledge. It built judgement about what should be automated, what must remain human, and how to make a useful system understandable to the person who has to rely on it.

Now that work can become something practical: a New Zealand business product that makes useful AI available beyond enterprise budgets. The point is not to remove value from the people building it. The point is to stop pretending ordinary businesses need to be priced out of the future.
